QA coordination in agile is where many delivery plans quietly break. I started my career in QA, so I have seen this from both sides. Teams plan features with confidence and treat testing as something that happens in a gap at the end. Then quality surprises arrive late, and the release date moves. All of it is avoidable.

## Put QA at the planning table

The testing voice belongs in planning, not just in standups. When I open a sprint, I ask what the team will test, how, and with what data. If nobody can answer, the story is not ready — regardless of how clear the code looks.

## Make "done" mean tested

The simplest fix for quality problems is a definition of done that explicitly includes testing. A feature that is coded but untested is not done. It is a risk with a name. I apply that phrase consistently until it stops sounding like a rule and starts sounding like common sense.

## Protect testing time on purpose

Testing time disappears first when pressure rises. I schedule it like development time, with the same respect. When a sprint gets heavy, I would rather cut a feature than absorb its testing time into the last two days of the release.

## Report on quality, not just features

A status update that says "all features complete" is incomplete. I report on what is tested, what has defects, and what the remaining risk is. Project managers who only track feature percentage are describing a plan, not the product.

## Use defect data to improve, not blame

Defects are information. When a pattern of defects shows up — say, regressions in the same module — I treat it as a signal about the requirement, the design, or the test strategy. The goal is to prevent the next one, not to assign blame for the last one.

## Keep one source of truth for release readiness

At release time I want a single view: what is ready, what is blocked, and what is acceptable risk. That view lets the team and the client make one honest decision instead of several hopeful ones.

## Key takeaway

QA coordination in agile works when testing is woven into the whole rhythm of a sprint instead of bolted on at the end. Give QA a seat in planning, make done mean tested, protect the time, and report on quality honestly. Delivery gets more predictable at almost no extra cost.

**Coordination checklist:**
- QA is represented during sprint planning
- Definition of done includes testing
- Testing time is scheduled like dev time
- Status updates include quality position
- Defect patterns drive process changes
- Release readiness lives in one visible place
